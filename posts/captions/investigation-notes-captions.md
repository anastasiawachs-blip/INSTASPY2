## 10 — Verify the license before you hire

Before hiring a private investigator in Florida, check the license yourself.

A private investigative agency should hold a Class A license. An individual investigator should hold a Class C license. The Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services provides a public license search—use it, and confirm that the name and status match what you were given.

Do not rely only on a website, business card or license number shown in an advertisement.

## 11 — Keep the original evidence

If a message, profile, listing or document may matter later, save the original first.

For online material, include the account name, full URL when possible, and the date and time. Keep the untouched file and make a separate copy for highlighting or cropping. The information outside the cropped area may become important later.

#EvidencePreservation #InvestigationTip #DigitalEvidence

## 12 — Start with the decision at hand

Tell the investigator exactly what you are trying to find out.

Checking a potential business partner is different from screening a household employee or locating someone. The reason for the investigation affects which records can be used and how the work should be handled.

The more specific the question, the more useful the result.

#DueDiligence #BackgroundInvestigation #RiskManagement

## 13 — Better identifiers. Fewer false matches.

A name by itself is often not enough to identify the correct person.

Provide whatever you lawfully have: full legal name, middle name, approximate age, former city, employer, known company or alias. Even one additional detail can prevent a record belonging to someone else from being included.

The same name does not mean the same person.

#BackgroundCheck #IdentityResearch #InvestigationTip

## 14 — A company record is a starting point

Sunbiz can show whether a Florida company is registered and who is listed in its filings. It does not prove that the business is legitimate or operating as represented.

Check the people behind the company, its address, required licenses, court history and related businesses. Compare those records with what the company told you.

Company registration is only one part of the check.

#BusinessDueDiligence #Sunbiz #CorporateResearch

## 15 — Ask who will actually enter your home

Before allowing a caregiver, contractor, housekeeper or other worker into your home, ask who will actually arrive.

Companies may send a substitute or subcontractor. Confirm the person’s name in advance and check identification when they arrive. For licensed work, also verify the applicable license and insurance.

Checking the company name alone is not enough.

#ResidentialSecurity #VendorScreening #NaplesFlorida

## 16 — No honest asset search promises everything

No legitimate investigator can guarantee that every asset will be found.

An asset search may identify real estate, business interests, liens, judgments and other records. Some assets may be held through another person or entity, kept outside the searched area, or simply not appear in public and lawful databases.

A good report clearly explains what was confirmed and what remains unknown.

#AssetSearch #FinancialInvestigation #DueDiligence

## 17 — Title is only one layer

The deed and title search do not tell you everything about a property.

Also check permits, open code-enforcement matters, recorded liens, foreclosure history and lawsuits involving the property or owner. Compare those records with the listing and documents you were given.

This is especially important when buying distressed property or purchasing through a company.

#PropertyInvestigation #RealEstateDueDiligence #NaplesRealEstate

## 18 — Start with the official state search

Before paying anyone to locate unclaimed property, search Florida’s official site yourself.

FLTreasureHunt.gov is free, and you can submit your own claim without paying a representative. Outside help may be useful when the claim involves an estate, business, several beneficiaries, missing records or a claim that was previously closed without payment.

Ask what work will be performed and what fee will be charged before signing an agreement.
